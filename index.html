The Founder Trap

The patterns that show up
in every founder-dependent business

Not unique to your industry. Not your fault. But completely solvable — if you know what you're building toward.

Pattern 01
Every decision routes through you
You have a team. But approvals, judgment calls, and anything non-routine still land on your desk. You're not the owner anymore — you're the system.
Pattern 02
More revenue, less freedom
The numbers are growing. So are the hours. You've built something that works — but only because you're in it constantly. That's not a business. That's a job with more responsibility.
Pattern 03
Hiring creates more chaos
Every person you add needs direction, coordination, and decisions from you. Without a system underneath them, more headcount just means more to manage.
Pattern 04
It all lives in your head
No playbooks. No documented process. The business runs on your memory and your relationships. You can't sell it, can't step back from it. That's not equity — that's a trap.

Proprietary Framework

Every engagement follows the same five-phase methodology. Diagnostic-first, build-second, optimize-always. No generic frameworks dropped on your business from the outside — built from the inside, together.

Phase 01
Reveal
Full diagnostic of your business: revenue by source, where time is going, how decisions flow, where margin is leaking. We map what you actually have.
↳ Business Audit Report
Phase 02
Architect
Design the org chart, define every role, clarify ownership of outcomes. Who owns what, who decides what, where authority lives. Built on paper first.
↳ Org Design + Role Map
Phase 03
Engineer
Build the actual operating system: weekly rhythms, SOPs, reporting structures, communication protocols. The machine that runs when you're not in the room.
↳ Full Ops Playbook
Phase 04
Install
Train your team on the system. Embed the rhythms. Activate the metrics dashboard. Design becomes daily reality — not a document that sits in a folder.
↳ Team Training + Launch
Phase 05
Optimize
Monthly retainer. Review KPIs, troubleshoot friction, evolve the system as you grow. The architecture is never done — it scales with you.
↳ Monthly Ops Sessions

What kind of businesses do you work with?+
Primarily service businesses — agencies, consulting firms, professional services, sales-driven teams, CRM-based businesses, and any founder-led company with recurring operations and a growing team. The industry matters less than the pattern: a founder who's become the bottleneck, a team that can't move without them, and a business that has outgrown how it was built.
What's the difference between this and hiring a COO?+
A full-time COO costs $150K–$250K/year and takes 3–6 months to ramp. A fractional arrangement gives you the same strategic thinking and systems design at a fraction of the cost, with no long-term employment commitment. You also get someone who has been a founder — not just a corporate exec — which changes the quality of the advice. When you're ready for a full-time hire, I can help you build the role and find the right person.
What does the 30-day Architecture Sprint actually look like?+
Week 1: Diagnostic — we audit your revenue, time allocation, org structure, and decision-making. Week 2: Architecture — we design the org chart, role clarity, and ownership map. Week 3: Engineering — we build the playbooks, SOPs, cadences, and KPI framework. Week 4: Installation — we present to your team, train the system, and hand off with confidence. Two working sessions per week throughout, plus async access.
I think I'm leaving money on the table. Can you help find where?+
Yes — and this is one of the most common things I surface in the diagnostic phase. Revenue leaks usually happen in a few predictable places: no follow-up system, inconsistent client experience, pricing that doesn't hold, or deals falling through because no one owns the process end-to-end. The audit typically finds two or three specific leaks within the first two weeks.
Can we skip the Sprint and go straight to retainer?+
In rare cases — if you have existing, well-documented systems I can audit quickly. But usually, trying to optimize a system that hasn't been properly architected is like renovating without blueprints. The Sprint is where the foundation gets built, and it typically pays for itself within the first month of retainer work.
How many clients do you take at once?+
Maximum of 4 retainer clients at any given time. This isn't a marketing constraint — it's a real one. The depth of work I do requires genuine attention and bandwidth. If the roster is full, I'll tell you directly and we can talk about timing.
What if I'm not sure the timing is right?+
Book a 30-minute call. No pitch, no pressure. We'll talk about where your business is, what's breaking, and whether there's a fit. If there isn't, I'll say so and point you somewhere more useful. The best time to build systems is almost always before you're completely overwhelmed by the need for them.
