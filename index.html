The Premise

Systems don’t kill culture.
Bad systems do.

Most ops consultants come from corporate. They hand you a playbook built for a company that looks nothing like yours — and wonder why it doesn’t fit. Your business isn’t a template. It shouldn’t run like one.

The culture you’ve built — the tight-knit team, the organic relationships, the way your clients feel when they work with you — that’s not a liability to work around. That’s the thing worth protecting.

My job is to build the operational layer underneath your culture. The systems, the rhythms, the infrastructure that lets your business run like itself — just without you being the only thing holding it together.

I’ve been the founder who couldn’t take a vacation. Who hired people only to become their full-time manager. I know what it costs to grow without infrastructure. And I know what it looks like when you get it right.

“The goal isn’t to make your business run like a corporation. It’s to make it run like itself — without you in every room.”
The Patterns

What it looks like when a founder-led business outgrows itself

These aren’t signs you built something wrong. They’re signs you built something real — and it’s ready for the next layer.

Pattern 01

Every decision still routes through you

You have a team. But the judgment calls, the approvals, the anything-unusual still lands on your desk. You’re not a founder anymore — you’re a system of one.

Pattern 02

The culture is strong but fragile

What makes your team special lives in relationships, vibes, and your presence. The moment you step back or add headcount — it starts to slip. Because nothing was ever written down.

Pattern 03

Hiring makes you busier, not freer

Every person you bring on needs you to orient them, answer their questions, make the calls they can’t yet make. Without a system underneath them, more headcount just means more to manage.

Pattern 04

The whole thing lives in your head

No playbooks. No documented process. The business runs on your memory, your instincts, your relationships. You can’t step back because no one else knows how it works.

Common Questions

Still have questions?

Most things get answered in a 15-minute call. But here are the ones that come up most.

Book a Call →
Will this change the culture we’ve built?
+

This is the most important question — and the honest answer is: not if we do it right. The whole premise of this work is building operational infrastructure underneath your culture, not over it. In Phase 03, we actually produce a Culture + Operating Principles document — the unwritten rules, the values in practice, the way decisions actually get made — codified so they survive growth and new hires. Your culture doesn’t get flattened. It gets protected.

My business is pretty unique. Will a generic framework fit?
+

It won’t — which is why I don’t use one. The Founder Independence Method is a process, not a template. Every engagement starts with a full diagnostic of your specific business. Everything we build comes from that. I’ve worked with boutique service businesses, niche creative studios, tight-knit sales teams, and community-based companies. The pattern is always the same. The solution never is.

Why don’t you list pricing on the site?
+

Because the right scope depends entirely on what your business actually needs — and I can’t know that before we talk. A 5-person studio with a culture fragility problem needs something different than a 12-person agency where revenue is leaking and the founder is doing everything. Pricing after a conversation means you’re paying for what you actually need. Engagements are typically structured as a focused build followed by ongoing retainer support. We figure out the exact shape together.

What’s the difference between this and hiring a COO?
+

A full-time COO costs $150K–$250K/year, takes 3–6 months to ramp, and is a long-term employment commitment. A fractional arrangement gives you the same strategic thinking and systems design at a fraction of the cost, with no long-term commitment. You also get someone who has been a founder — not just a corporate exec — which changes the quality of the work entirely.

What kind of businesses do you work with?
+

Primarily service businesses — agencies, studios, consulting firms, sales-driven teams, and founder-led companies with recurring operations and a growing team. The industry matters less than the pattern: a founder who’s become the bottleneck, a team that can’t move without them, and a business that’s outgrown how it was built.

How long does it take to see results?
+

The diagnostic phase alone usually surfaces two or three things that can be fixed immediately — within the first two weeks. The full build takes 30 days. Most founders start feeling the difference quickly: clearer decision-making, fewer things landing on their desk, team moving with more confidence. The deeper freedom — actually stepping back — typically comes in months two and three.
